CollabASM: A Novel Benchmark Dataset for Human-Human and Human-Robot Collaborative Shelf Assembly Tasks

1The University of Tokyo  2Tokyo Denki University  3RIKEN AIP

Abstract

Collaborative motion is crucial in daily life and industrial contexts, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks. Despite advances in autonomous agents collaborating with characters in virtual environments and humanoids in the real world, achieving seamless collaboration remains a challenge. A major obstacle is developing models that accurately predict human or character behaviors while considering mutual influences to optimize task performance. Many existing datasets for training human motion prediction models focus only on individual behaviors and lack scalability for collaborative tasks, with few supporting the transition from human-human to human-robot collaboration.

This study introduces CollabASM: a benchmark dataset for human-human and human-robot collaborative shelf assembly tasks that captures motion and sensory information for both types of collaboration. The dataset includes detailed human and robot motions, finger motions, allocentric and egocentric videos, and multi-level task annotations. It covers the assembly and disassembly of 14 furniture items and 5 composite variations. Our experiments demonstrate the dataset's utility as a scalable benchmark for transitioning from human-human to human-robot collaboration under a teleoperated setup.

CollabASM captures both Human-Human and Human-Robot collaborative shelf assembly, including full-body motion, finger motion, egocentric/allocentric RGB-D video, and multi-level task annotations.

Key Features

CollabASM is the first corpus that simultaneously satisfies all of the following key properties:

  • Long-horizon assembly tasks — assembly and disassembly of 14 furniture items and 5 composite variations.
  • Full finger-inclusive joint motion tracking — captured via tracking gloves integrated with motion capture.
  • Multi-level action and task annotations — three levels of abstraction (fine-grained action labels, furniture-specific task labels, and common task labels).
  • Paired Human-Human (H-H) and Human-Robot (H-R) execution conditions.
  • Identical tasks performed across both interaction modes.

The dataset comprises 401 recorded sequences (25 hours total; 18.1 hours H-H and 6.9 hours H-R) with synchronized, multimodal signals: full-body motion capture, finger motion via tracking gloves, multi-view allocentric RGB-D, egocentric RGB, robot joint and end-effector trajectory, and robot egocentric video. H-R interactions are SpaceMouse-teleoperated, providing a baseline for human-in-the-loop teleoperation and enabling future autonomous policy learning.
